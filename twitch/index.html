What should you charge for a sponsored stream?

Streams are not priced per view. They are priced per concurrent viewer per hour, and that changes everything.

Figures gathered 24 July 2026 from published benchmark data, cross-checked against deals creators and brand-side buyers report publicly. Full method and sources.

The underlying figure streamers report is $0.50 to $1.00 per concurrent viewer per hour. One reported a flat $300 for a three-hour indie game sponsorship; another described $10 to $13 an hour at 13 concurrents, rising to about $20 an hour from a larger company.

Why per hour and not per view

A sponsor buying a video is buying something that accumulates views for months. A sponsor buying a stream is buying a block of live attention that happens once. The two are priced on completely different bases, and applying video CPMs to a stream produces a number that is wrong by an order of magnitude in either direction depending on your size.

Your follower count matters even less here than elsewhere. Concurrent viewers is the entire metric.

Do not price a stream like a video

A calculator built for feed posts will tell a 20-concurrent streamer to ask for a video-sized minimum, they will quote it, and they will get silence. The floor for streaming is genuinely lower: around $25 for an hour rising to about $100 for a stream bundled with social posts.

That is not underselling yourself. It is what the market for that audience size actually pays, and quoting five times it gets you nothing rather than more.

What still applies

Usage rights, exclusivity and rush timelines work the same as anywhere else. If a sponsor wants to clip your stream and run the clip as an ad, that is a usage licence and it is charged separately from the stream fee.
