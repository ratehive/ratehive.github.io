What CPM should you charge for a YouTube sponsorship?

Your niche changes this by a factor of three. Almost no rate guide says so.

Figures gathered 24 July 2026 from published benchmark data, cross-checked against deals creators and brand-side buyers report publicly. Full method and sources.

Same views, same engagement, same work. A finance channel is worth roughly two and a half times a lifestyle channel of identical size, because advertisers pay for how commercially valuable the audience is rather than how many people are in it.

If you are in a premium niche and quoting lifestyle rates, that is the single most expensive mistake available to you.

Gaming deserves a specific warning

A brand manager who says they have placed around $2m in creator deals puts Minecraft, Fortnite and Roblox content at $15 to $25 CPM and notes that many brands decline the category outright, whatever the audience size.

That is not a reflection of the work. It is that a large share of the audience is assumed to be children, and most advertisers will not buy against it at any price.

If a brand asks for "a quick mention" and then sends a two-minute script, that is a different job at a different price. Say so before you agree a number.

Subscriber count does not matter. Views do.

The highest-voted answer in the largest thread on this asks you to take your average views, divide by a thousand, and multiply by your CPM — and states flatly that sub count does not matter for sponsors.

Practitioners consistently name $15 to $30 CPM for a general channel, $25 to $35 for a decent niche, and $40 to $65 where the audience is genuinely valuable. Those figures line up with the published benchmarks above, which is the main reason to trust either.

A channel with 330,000 subscribers averaging 8,000 views is worth roughly what 8,000 views is worth, not what 330,000 subscribers sounds like it should be.
