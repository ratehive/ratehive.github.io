Where these numbers come from

Because a rate calculator that won't show its working is just a number generator.

The base is a CPM, a price per thousand views. Each platform and deliverable has a baseline rate for a general or lifestyle audience. YouTube long-form integration sits at $20, which is the documented midpoint for lifestyle channels. A dedicated video is worth about 1.4x an integration and a Short about 0.6x, so those follow from it.

Niche moves that base more than anything else. Documented YouTube integration CPMs run $15–25 for lifestyle and $40–80 for B2B and developer tools. That's a threefold spread, and it's the single biggest reason a generic calculator gives you a wrong number. Finance sits around 2.25x lifestyle, tech reviews 1.75x, beauty 1.35x, gaming 1.15x.

Views matter far more than follower count. This is the most consistent thing creators and brand-side managers say, and most rate advice gets it wrong. One agency manager put it plainly: a creator with 100k followers averaging 2k views will take $500, while one with 30k followers averaging 40k views is worth considerably more. There's still a floor based on audience size, so a bad month doesn't tell you to work for nothing, but it's capped at twice the view estimate. A large dormant audience does not get quoted like a live one.

It's checked against what creators actually report. Practitioners on r/PartneredYoutube and r/InfluencerMarketing consistently name $15–30 CPM for general YouTube channels, $25–35 for a decent niche, and $40–65 where the niche is genuinely valuable. One brand manager who has placed around $2m in deals gave $30 CPM for on-camera commentary and $15–25 for Minecraft, Fortnite or Roblox content, which many brands decline outright. Those figures line up with the published benchmarks, which is the main reason to trust either.

Engagement is judged against your own platform and size, not a fixed number. 4% is strong on Instagram and slightly below par on TikTok, and engagement reliably falls as accounts grow, so a 5k account and a 500k account are held to different standards. Typical figures used: Instagram 4.5% / 2.5% / 1.2% and TikTok 6.5% / 4.5% / 3.0% across nano, micro and macro.

Usage rights, exclusivity and whitelisting are multipliers, in line with reported industry practice: usage adds 20–50% of base per month of use with perpetual rights running 100–300%, whitelisting around 30% a month, exclusivity 25–100% depending on how broadly it's drawn.

UGC is handled separately. The market median is about $175 a video and it's priced on production and experience rather than audience, so follower count is capped hard and niche is damped.

What it can't do. There's no geography input, and rates outside the US and UK differ a lot. It can't know your track record, which matters as much as audience size once you have one. The niche figures are derived from YouTube data and extrapolated to other platforms, which is directionally right but still an extrapolation. And it takes your follower count on trust.

One thing worth knowing before you reply to anyone. Creators repeatedly report that cold inbound offers land at $150–200 regardless of what they're worth, and that brands fishing at that level rarely move much. A creator with 9–12% engagement described being offered $150–200 for multiple videos. If your number and their number are far apart, that is often a sign you're talking to the wrong brand rather than that you're priced wrong.

If you've done a deal that came out a long way from what this says, that's the useful information, not a bug. The numbers get revised when real ones contradict them.
