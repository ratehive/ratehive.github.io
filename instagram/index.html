What should you charge for a sponsored Instagram post?

Rates by follower tier and format, the CPM they are built from, and the one number most rate guides leave out.

Figures gathered 24 July 2026 from published benchmark data, cross-checked against deals creators and brand-side buyers report publicly. Full method and sources.

Rates by follower tier

Published asking rates, and reported actual spend beside them.

Account sizeFeed postStory frame
Under 10,000 followers$100–500$50–200
10,000 to 100,000$500–5,000 asking$200–1,000
Over 100,000$5,000–50,000$1,000–10,000

The second row is where most creators sit and where the published figures are most misleading. Asking rates for a 10,000 to 100,000 follower account run $500 to $5,000 a feed post, but reported actual brand spend at that size averages $150 to $800. Both numbers are real. They are just answering different questions, and almost every rate guide quotes only the first.

Reels command roughly 1.5 to 3 times a static feed post. That premium is real and worth quoting separately rather than folding into one blended rate.

What the numbers are built from

Those are baselines for a general or lifestyle audience. A beauty or fashion account runs about 1.35x higher, finance around 2.25x, and B2B roughly 3x. Niche moves the number more than follower count does.

Views matter more than followers

The most consistent thing brand-side buyers say, and the thing most creators get wrong when quoting. One agency manager put it plainly: a creator with 100,000 followers averaging 2,000 views will take around $500, while one with 30,000 followers averaging 40,000 views is worth considerably more.

If your views are well below your follower count, quoting from followers gets you ignored. If they are well above it, quoting from followers costs you money.

Engagement, in context

What counts as good depends on your size.

Instagram averages about 0.98% engagement by follower count, with Reels nearer 3.8%. By tier, nano accounts under 10,000 followers typically run 3.5–6% on static posts and 5–9% on Reels; between 10,000 and 100,000 it falls to 1.5–3.5% and 3–5%.

Engagement reliably drops as accounts grow, so a 5,000-follower account and a 500,000-follower account should not be held to the same number.
